### Substance and truth rules (added 2026-05-25 from a review of "The Pin Factory Trap")

- **Anecdote specifics must be literally true.** Do not invent a time marker ("I had breakfast today," "last week") if it is not accurate, and do not inflate a person or thing with an untrue superlative ("one of the most earnest people in science I know"). Use the true detail, a vaguer but true detail, or no detail. Truth ranks above texture.

- **Cut filler and decorative sentences.** Remove any sentence that carries no information, argument, or transition. "And it had lodged in my head in a way I could not shake" is filler. If cutting a sentence loses no idea, cut it.

- **Finish every thought. No vague trailing gestures.** Do not point at something without naming it. "What happens when specialisation curdles into something else" leaves the reader asking "into what?". Name it, or cut the clause.

- **No throat-clearing or writing about the writing.** Skip lines that announce the essay instead of advancing it ("This essay is me trying to work out what that collision means"). Make the point rather than narrating the intention to make it.

- **Substance-density test (the 20% rule).** A finished piece should not be compressible by more than about 20% without losing actual intellectual content. Run a compression pass before publishing: if a third of a paragraph can go with no loss of idea, it was padded. Be direct. The default is substance, not writing for the sake of writing.

- **Every sentence must do work (the "saying a lot but not much" test, added 2026-05-28).** The truest AI tell is volume with low signal: sentences that sound substantive but only restate the previous line, decorate it, or warm up the next one. The piece reads smooth and full and ends up saying nothing. Read each sentence in isolation and name what it adds, a new piece of information, an argument move, or a load-bearing transition. If a sentence only reinforces what came before or sets up what comes after, cut it. The previous sentence already carried its weight, and the next sentence can set its own context. A draft of 6 substantive sentences beats a draft of 12 where half are echo. This is finer-grained than the filler rule (whole-sentence binary) and the 20% rule (whole-piece compressibility): it forces the per-sentence question "what does THIS one say that the others do not".

### Style refinements (added 2026-05-25, from Isaac's edits to the hyper-specialisation essay)

- **Numbers as numerals.** Write numbers as digits (18, 99%, 15 years, the 2 adages, 48,000), including most cardinals and ordinals, rather than spelling them out. Three exceptions: a number that would open a sentence (rephrase so it does not), numbers inside a direct quotation (keep the quote verbatim), and an idiomatic "one" that means "a / a single" rather than a count (one machine, one foot, one corner). This pushes the older "concrete numbers" note in Section 6 firmly toward digits for Substack.

- **Prefer connectors over stacked appositives.** Do not let the comma-offset appositive be the default way to join a thought. Where it reads better, carry the logic with a connector (yet, however, so, because, which). Isaac actively rewrites toward this ("Yet...", "Worse still...", "Now it seems...").

- **Enrich anecdotes with true, specific detail.** He adds concrete facts to the hook (the secret experiments, the Cambridge breakthrough, the state-funded lab). Prefer specific true detail over generic phrasing, and leave clearly marked slots for him to drop in facts only he knows.

- **Anchor with real aphorisms.** Within the 2 to 3 named-anchor ceiling, a well-chosen quote from a known thinker is on-brand (Munger on incentives, Taleb on the monthly salary as an addiction).

- **The spaced hyphen " - " is allowed.** It is a hyphen, not an em or en dash, and Isaac uses it as a connector in published prose. Do not strip it. The em-dash and en-dash ban still stands.

- **The close can be a plain playbook, not only an open question.** A mildly prescriptive ending ("this looks like the playbook: ...") is acceptable alongside the sober, unresolved close.

### AI-tell purge (added 2026-05-26, after the "quietly erodes" catch on the CPF line)

The voice cannot read like a chatbot. If a word or pattern is one of the top tells of LLM writing, default to cutting it. This list sits on top of every rule above. The 99% target applies: scrub 99% of these from any draft before it ships.

- **Adverbs to strike on sight: quietly, subtly, carefully, seamlessly, effectively, strategically, consistently, additionally, essentially, ultimately, importantly, notably, significantly, increasingly.** These adverbs do a sentence's work without earning it. "What it actually buys quietly erodes" was the trigger example: the adverb hides the missing mechanism. Replace with the concrete cause, or drop the adverb and let the verb carry the load.

- **Verbs and nouns to cut: delve, navigate (in the abstract sense, as in "navigate the landscape"), leverage (as filler verb), utilize, foster, streamline, unlock, harness, embark, underscore, robust, pivotal, crucial, comprehensive, multifaceted, seamless, landscape (as filler noun), realm, tapestry, beacon, treasure trove, synergy, journey (as metaphor), ecosystem (as metaphor).** Use the literal word: "use" rather than "utilize", "world" or the actual market rather than "landscape", "wide-ranging" rather than "multifaceted".

- **Phrases to never write: "It is important to note", "In conclusion", "In essence", "It is worth noting", "Needless to say", "At the end of the day", "When it comes to", "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving world of".** A claim either matters and gets stated, or it does not and gets cut. The framing itself is the tell.

- **Sentence openers to never use: "Certainly", "Absolutely", "Indeed", "Moreover", "Furthermore".** As a sentence opener these mark the text as machine-generated.

- **"The honest X is Y" framing is banned (added 2026-05-29).** "The honest answer is yes", "the honest pitch is automation", "the honest take is", any variant. The word "honest" used this way is editorialising, the LLM signalling that it has cut through the noise to give you the real version. State the claim directly: "The answer is yes", "the pitch is automation", "the take is". The reader assumes you are being honest already; flagging it as such is the tell.

- **Single-sentence contrastive reframe ("not just X, but Y" / "not only X, but also Y" / "more than just X, it is Y") is banned too.** The existing negation-reframe rule kills the 2-sentence version. This is its single-sentence form and reads the same way. State Y directly.

- **The trailing "whether" close.** LLMs often end a paragraph or essay on a "whether X or Y" summary line. End on the actual position, the actual question, or the actual silence.

- **Suppress the rule-of-three reflex.** LLMs default to triplets (adjective, adjective, adjective, or short phrase, short phrase, short phrase) as a way to sound balanced. Cap at one triplet per piece, and only when each item is doing distinct work.

- **The 99% rule.** This list is not exhaustive. If a word or move feels like the kind of thing ChatGPT would reach for, that is itself the signal to cut. Treat the discomfort of removing a smooth-sounding adverb or transition as a feature, the rough edge is the human signature.

## 12. The Humanizer (mandatory QA pass on every draft, every platform)

Every draft, every platform (X, LinkedIn, Substack, Instagram, ad-hoc) must pass the Humanizer before it is saved as Draft in Notion. The Humanizer is a single linear pass over the draft that strips chatbot tells. If any check fails, fix the line and re-run. No exceptions, no shortcuts.

The Humanizer is the consolidation of every rule in Section 11 that exists to make the writing sound human rather than machine. The remaining Section 11 rules (Oxford comma drop, numbers as numerals, spaced hyphen, real aphorisms, playbook close) are Style rules: they shape the voice, not the human-ness. Style rules are still binding, they just are not what the Humanizer is for.

### What the Humanizer checks

**1. Punctuation and structural tells**

- No em-dash or en-dash anywhere (the "—" or "–" characters). Spaced hyphen " - " is fine.

- No anaphora (same word starting consecutive sentences or clauses).

- No 2-sentence negation-reframe ("It is not X. It is Y."). State Y directly.

- No single-sentence contrastive reframe ("not just X, but Y" / "not only X, but also Y" / "more than just X, it is Y").

- No paragraph or essay ending on a "whether X or Y" summary line.

- Cap rule-of-three triplets at 1 per piece, and only when each item is doing distinct work.

- No new metaphors or analogies. Reuse only Isaac's existing ones (pin factory, Plato's cave, air-conditioned cage, parkour leap, basketball, fractal). If a fresh comparison tempts, state the idea literally.

**2. Vocabulary blacklist (the AI-tell purge list)**

- Adverbs to strike on sight: quietly, subtly, carefully, seamlessly, effectively, strategically, consistently, additionally, essentially, ultimately, importantly, notably, significantly, increasingly.

- Verbs and nouns to cut: delve, navigate (abstract sense), leverage (as filler), utilize, foster, streamline, unlock, harness, embark, underscore, robust, pivotal, crucial, comprehensive, multifaceted, seamless, landscape (as filler noun), realm, tapestry, beacon, treasure trove, synergy, journey (as metaphor), ecosystem (as metaphor).

- Phrases to never write: "It is important to note", "In conclusion", "In essence", "It is worth noting", "Needless to say", "At the end of the day", "When it comes to", "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving world of", "The honest answer is", "The honest pitch is", "The honest take is" (and any other "The honest X is Y" framing). State the claim directly without flagging it as honest.

- Sentence openers to never use: "Certainly", "Absolutely", "Indeed", "Moreover", "Furthermore".

**3. Substance and texture**

- Every sentence must do work (the "saying a lot but not much" test). The truest AI tell is volume with low signal: each line sounds substantive but only restates, decorates, or warms up the next one. Read every sentence in isolation and name what it adds, a new piece of information, an argument move, or a load-bearing transition. If the sentence only reinforces the previous line or sets up the next, cut it. Six substantive sentences beat twelve where half are echo.

- No filler or decorative sentences. If cutting a sentence loses no idea, cut it.

- No throat-clearing or writing about the writing.

- No vague trailing gestures. Every thought finishes; every "something" is named.

- Every anecdote specific is literally true. No invented time markers, no inflated superlatives. Use a vaguer but true detail, or no detail.

- Substance density: a finished piece should not be compressible by more than ~20% without losing intellectual content. Run a compression pass.

- Prefer connectors (yet, however, so, because, which) over stacked comma-offset appositives.

**4. The 99% rule (final sweep)**

Read the draft once more, slowly. If a sentence sounds smooth in a way Isaac would not write, the smoothness IS the tell. Cut it or rough it up. The discomfort of a rougher line is the human signature. Target: 99% of the patterns above removed from any draft before it ships.

What it adds beyond the old word-list grep, drawn from the 2026 research pass on how AI text is actually detected (the burstiness literature, plus the Bouchard and GPTZero writer guides):

- **Paragraph rhythm.** Flags paragraphs that are too even in length. Let one run long and cut another to a couple of lines.

- **Transition density.** Counts machine-glue transitions (however, moreover, furthermore, additionally, thus, therefore and the rest) per 100 sentences, target 6 or fewer.

## 12. Reply Voice (X replies and comments only)

*Added 2026-05-26. Replies are not micro-essays. They are punches. The archetypes in Section 5 govern primary posts and long-form. Replies (on X, IG comments, LinkedIn comments) follow a stricter, narrower set of rules.*

**Rules (all five hold every time):**

- **No metaphors. No analogies.** No "like X", no "is the parkour leap of Y", no "the air-conditioned cage". State the claim flat. The Section 11 ban on new metaphors already covers this; the reply context tightens it to also exclude reuse of Isaac's own established analogies. Replies stay literal.

- **Super direct.** Subject, verb, indictment. No throat-clearing. No "interesting point but", no "I'd gently push back". Say the thing.

- **Contrarian punchline at the end, but FLAT.** The final sentence flips the framing, names the self-serving angle, or says what the existing replies are too polite to say. **No inverted, mirrored, or parallel-structure punchlines.** "Calling immigration the problem here is calling the fix the failure", "what's bursting is the middle, not the bubble", "the apocalypse was a B2B funnel, not a forecast", "the market is paying capital twice, once for substitution and once for access" all read as comedian-grade essay zingers and Isaac flagged them as AI-tell on 2026-05-28. Land the flip with a plain declarative sentence in conversational voice. Casual qualifiers ("unfortunately", "obviously", "for now") help it read as a person talking. If the flip cannot be said flat, omit it and stop at 2 sentences. Example: "calling immigration the problem here is calling the fix the failure" becomes "immigration is the only fix, unfortunately".

- **Three sentences maximum. Often two.** If a reply needs more, the angle is not sharp enough yet. Rewrite.

- All Section 11 hard rules still apply: no em-dashes or en-dashes, no anaphora, no negation-reframe, no AI-tell adverbs, British and Singaporean spelling.

- **Lowercase except names and standard-capitalised abbreviations.** (Added 2026-05-27, revised same day.) Every X reply Isaac writes is lowercase by default, EXCEPT: proper nouns keep their normal capitalisation (Singapore, OpenAI, Nvidia, LKY, Trump, Hong Kong, Anthropic) and standard-capitalised abbreviations keep theirs (AI, GPU, US, China, LLM, CEO, MDDI, HDB, CPF, NUS, USD, S$). Sentence starts that are NOT a proper noun go lowercase ("everything they ship...", not "Everything they ship..."). The pronoun "i" is lowercase. Punctuation stays normal. Direct quotes from external sources keep their original casing. This rule is X-only and does not extend to LinkedIn, Substack or IG.

**Exemplar (the bar Isaac set):**

> A fund wrote a $30M cheque to Polsia. The market is now paying a premium for capital without labour, before anyone has shown it actually works at scale. And capital without labor looks extremely self-serving, probably a bad name for capitalism.

Three sentences. Zero metaphors. The third sentence is the flip ("a bad name for capitalism"). Direct claims throughout. No hedging.

**Anti-patterns to avoid:**

- Metaphor reach: "this is parkour logic of capital, mid-leap..."

- Hedged opener: "I'd gently push back here, the framing might be incomplete because..."

- Tidy bow close: "Worth thinking about which kind of utility we are walking into."

- Question back to the OP: "What do you make of that?" (unless the question itself is the contrarian flip)

- Four or more sentences.

**Where this applies:** every "Suggested reply" line in the daily X Reply Radar, every comment Isaac drafts on someone else's post on X, IG, or LinkedIn. Long-form Substack and Isaac's own primary posts still allow Section 5 archetype E (the analogy lock) and reuse of his established figures. Reply Voice is stricter.

## 13. X Primary Voice (observer register)

*Added 2026-05-27, after Isaac corrected a draft that opened verdict-first ("OpenAI's Singapore lab is mostly hype. Nvidia's isn't.") as too direct. The shape below is the corrected version he kept and confirmed as the voice for all primary X posts going forward.*

For every primary X post (Single or Thread), default to the **observer / wry register**, not the prosecutorial one. Verdict-first cold opens ("X is hype", "X is dead", "X is a fiction held in place by Y") read as too declarative for Isaac's voice, even when the claim underneath is correct. The wryer move is to open with observation, let the asymmetry or contradiction surface through the facts, register skepticism with a rhetorical question, and land the broader claim at the end.

**The shape:**

1. Open with what is actually happening (the announcement, the news, the scene). State the fact flat.

1. Lay one concrete signal that points one way ("Nvidia is already hiring AI engineers here").

1. Register skepticism about the contrast item with a rhetorical question or a wry beat ("OpenAI? 'Applied AI Lab', whatever that means").

1. Land the broader claim or the sober close at the end, not the start.

**Exemplar (the version Isaac confirmed as the bar):**

> Singapore announced the OpenAI and Nvidia deals together. Nvidia is already hiring AI engineers here. OpenAI? 'Applied AI Lab', whatever that means. everything OpenAI does seems pretty suspect.

Fact, fact, rhetorical question, broader claim. The skepticism is observed, not declared. The reader has to do the small step from observation to conclusion, and that small step is what makes the post feel like Isaac wrote it and not a take-machine. Note the casing on the last sentence: "everything" is lowercase because it is a common word at the start of a sentence, while OpenAI keeps its capitalisation as a proper noun. See the lowercase rule below.

**How this resolves the "thesis in the first line" rule from the X Daily Tweet Workflow:**

The frame leads in line 1 (the fact that sets up the asymmetry), and the thesis lands at the end. "Thesis-first" reads as "frame-first" in the observer register. The thesis is not buried, it is delivered after the reader has been walked through the evidence that makes it land.

**Anti-patterns (rewrite if a draft does any of these):**

- Verdict cold opens: "OpenAI's Singapore lab is mostly hype.", "your NUS degree is not an asset.", "Singapore's AI moment is contingent." Even when the underlying claim is right, the opener is too direct for the X register.

- Drafts where a common word at the start of a sentence is capitalised ("Everything they ship...", "The signal is..."): under the lowercase rule below, common-word sentence starts go lowercase. Proper nouns and standard-capitalised abbreviations at the start of a sentence stay capitalised (Singapore, OpenAI, AI).

- Declarative shouting before the evidence is on the page: state the news first, let the claim land at the end.

- Skipping the rhetorical question or the wry beat: the question is the on-brand move that registers skepticism without claiming a verdict.

- Doubling the verdict at top and bottom: pick one place for the claim to land, and make it the close.

**Where this applies:** every primary X post (Singles and the hook tweet of Threads). Reply Voice in Section 12 still governs replies and comments (where the contrarian flip belongs in the final sentence and the 3-sentence cap holds). LinkedIn drafts should default to the observer register too unless the angle genuinely needs the bluntness of a verdict opener; **the lowercase rule below does not extend to LinkedIn**. Long-form Substack essays still allow the more dialectical, thesis-first shape from the Section 5 archetypes and keep standard capitalisation.

**Lowercase rule (added 2026-05-27, X-only):**

Every primary X post Isaac writes is **lowercase only**. This is a hard rule, on the same level as the no-em-dash rule.

- Sentence starts: lowercase ("singapore announced...", not "Singapore announced...").

- The pronoun "i": lowercase ("i think...", not "I think...").

- Proper nouns: lowercase (singapore, openai, nvidia, lky, hdb, cpf, nus).

- Acronyms: lowercase (ai, gpu, us, china, llm, ceo, mddi).

- Currency: lowercase units (s$300m, not S$300M; usd, not USD).

- Punctuation stays normal: periods, commas, question marks, apostrophes, quotation marks, hyphens, parentheses, colons all unchanged.

- Direct quotes from external sources keep their original casing (quote them verbatim inside quotation marks). Everything Isaac writes himself is lowercase.

- Links and handles keep their actual casing (URLs, @handles).

**Scope:** X primary posts AND X replies (Section 12). Lowercase does NOT apply to LinkedIn posts or comments, Substack essays, Instagram captions, email, or any other surface. Those keep standard capitalisation.

## 14. IG Carousel Voice (Instagram carousel slides only)

*Added 2026-05-29, after Isaac corrected the first AI-cost-play carousel draft. The X observer voice in Section 13 governs X primary posts. The IG Carousel Voice below governs the slide text on @*[*isaac.com.sg*](http://isaac.com.sg/)* multi-slide carousels. The IG Caption Voice Reference page governs the accompanying caption.*

For every IG carousel, the slide-text register sits between Reply Voice (too clipped) and Substack essay (too unhurried). The shape below is the corrected shape Isaac kept and confirmed.

**The shape:**

1. **Slide 1 is a question hook.** Not a verdict. The opener is a curiosity question that makes the viewer swipe to find out the answer, in the voice of the target audience asking it about themselves ("Should I implement AI in my business?", "Is my degree still worth anything?", "Why is everyone losing money on AI?"). One sentence. The previous rule on the workflow doc that banned questions-as-hooks is **superseded by this section for IG carousels.**

1. **Every middle slide is around 3 sentences.** Not one. Not two. One- or two-sentence middle slides read as AI-generated because they skip the natural argument-building Isaac does in his own writing. The middle slides flesh the point out the way he would have written it himself: state the claim, qualify it, work through the implication.

1. **Use connectors to sound natural.** *But, and, so, yet, because, instead, especially.* The connectors carry the argument's logic. A middle slide without a connector reads as bullet-pointy and machine-flat. The brand voice's hard rule against anaphora still holds (no repeated word at the start of consecutive sentences), but connectors are not anaphora, they are the joinery of the argument.

1. **Keep his qualifying language.** *Seems, it seems that, theoretically, in theory, technically, probably, almost, rightly so, most, more often.* These hedges are part of Isaac's tone, not weakness. They signal he is thinking through the argument out loud rather than declaring from above it. A middle slide stripped of qualifiers reads as Claude over-confident; a middle slide with qualifiers reads as Isaac working through it.

1. **The last slide always bookends the first slide.** If Slide 1 was the question "Should I implement AI in my business?", the last slide answers it directly ("So back to the question. Should you implement AI in your business? Yes, but only when you can actually move, and only when your implementer is running the thing in their own business."). The loop close is mandatory.

**All Section 11 hard rules still apply, including:**

- No em-dashes or en-dashes

- No anaphora

- No 2-sentence negation-reframe and no single-sentence "X, not Y" reframe at sentence end

- No new metaphors. Isaac's existing ones only (pin factory, Plato's cave, air-conditioned cage, parkour leap, basketball, fractal). A metaphor Isaac coined himself in the carousel outline counts as his (for example, "betting on the horse before the race starts").

- The Section 12 Humanizer pass is mandatory before saving the slide texts as Draft.

- Numerals not words ("15 years" not "fifteen years"). British / Singaporean spelling.

**Anti-patterns to rewrite if a draft does any of these:**

- Slide 1 as a verdict ("AI's upside is capped at your expense line") instead of a question. The verdict is fine inside the carousel, not as the cold open.

- Middle slides at 1 or 2 sentences. The compression that works for X primary posts kills the carousel rhythm.

- Middle slides with no connectors, just declarative chunks. Reads as a slide deck written by ChatGPT.

- Middle slides stripped of every qualifier in the name of being "punchy". The qualifiers are the voice.

- Last slide that lands a fresh idea instead of closing the loop on Slide 1. The bookend is mandatory.

- Last slide that ends on a "follow for more" or "thoughts?" CTA. The bookend is the close.

**Where this applies:** every slide on every IG carousel on @[isaac.com.sg](http://isaac.com.sg/). Does not extend to the caption (governed by IG Caption Voice Reference), X primary posts (Section 13), X replies and comments (Section 12), LinkedIn (Section 4 tone-by-context matrix), or Substack (Section 5 archetypes). IG Story blurbs follow their own per-essay archetype (see the IG Story Workflow doc), not this section.
